# Business Recommendations

Consolidated from the Moulding and Assembly presentation decks. Each recommendation is stated as an option for the business to weigh, grounded in a specific finding — not presented as a settled conclusion, since several depend on operational context this analysis couldn't see from the data alone.

---

## Moulding Division

### 1. Investigate why 41% of machine-shifts are idle
This single factor explains most of the gap between the 42.2% headline OEE and the 71.6% operational figure. Is it order volume, scheduling, changeover capacity, or labour availability? Until this is understood, "OEE" as a single number will keep meaning two different things to two different audiences.

### 2. Study the top reliability performers, not just the worst
The best machine sustains ≥90% running efficiency on 87% of its shifts; the 15th-best manages 63%. Understanding what the top performers do differently — tooling, operator practice, maintenance cadence — is very likely a cheaper lever than diagnosing 41 separate underperforming machines individually.

### 3. Track material waste by weight, not by unit count
One material accounts for 53% of units produced but 81% of total material weight. A unit-based waste or scrap metric will systematically understate where cost actually leaks, since part weight varies roughly 35-fold across the 13 materials in use.

### 4. Protect uptime on the highest-volume tooling
Ten moulds carry 54% of total plant output, with the single largest at 13% alone. Preventative maintenance and spare-tooling priority on this small set has a disproportionate commercial return compared with spreading effort evenly across all moulds.

## Assembly Division

### 5. Treat micro-stoppages and major breakdowns as separate problems
One line stops roughly 41,000 times a period, averaging 3.4 minutes per stop; another stops only ~2,400 times, averaging 60 minutes. These are different failure modes — likely feed/jamming/handling on one, and breakdowns or long changeovers on the other — and are very unlikely to share a single fix.

### 6. Correct reject-count capture on the affected line
Waste in kilograms is being recorded on a line that simultaneously shows zero rejects on every shift — a measurement gap, not a genuine quality result. Until reject counts are captured, that line cannot be benchmarked against the rest of the plant on quality at all, and any ranking that includes it as-is will mislead.

### 7. Establish whether the lowest-utilised lines are under-scheduled or under-performing
The two worst lines on downtime also record the most shifts with zero output. If this reflects genuine spare capacity against current demand, it's a planning question; if it reflects a technical constraint, it's a maintenance one. The distinction changes what "improving" these lines would actually mean.

### 8. Prioritise service continuity for the concentrated customer base
20 of 95 customers account for 81% of booked volume, and a smaller group of 13 repeat customers (10+ orders each) account for 66% on their own. Disruption affecting these accounts carries materially more commercial weight than disruption spread evenly — worth reflecting in how maintenance and changeover priority is set across lines.

## Cross-divisional

### 9. Extend the Product Family link between Moulding and Assembly
Several Moulding tool descriptions share naming conventions with Assembly's product families, suggesting the two divisions could eventually be viewed as one connected pipeline rather than two separate reports. This was out of scope for the current analysis but is a natural next step once mould-to-family mapping is confirmed for the remaining, non-prefixed parts.

### 10. Separate "idle capacity" from "operational downtime" as distinct, permanently tracked measures
Both divisions show the same underlying pattern: a large share of recorded machine-time is idle rather than actively stopped mid-run, and blending the two into one OEE or downtime figure obscures two different management questions — how much capacity is being used at all, versus how well it runs once it is.
